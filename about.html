About

A short story

I am interested in using computational methods to study complex biological systems, particularly the brain, and in developing reliable tools for biomedical research.

My research interests span machine learning, computational neuroscience, and biomedical data science. Across these areas, I am drawn to questions about how biological patterns can be identified from data, how computational models behave under different conditions, and how research findings can be evaluated and reproduced.

My thesis in computational neuroscience, Experience-Dependent Reorganization and Stabilization of Population Neural Representations During Repeated Visual Exposure, examined how neural population representations change and stabilize with repeated visual exposure. I completed and defended the thesis, and the resulting manuscript is prepared for submission. This work deepened my interest in computational approaches to neuroscience and in understanding how patterns of neural activity relate to experience.

Alongside my academic work, I have developed software projects focused on biomedical data analysis and machine learning. These include a tool for investigating genetic variants, a pipeline for mortality prediction using clinical data, and an evaluation study of model performance under dataset shift. Working on these projects has led me to pay particular attention to data quality, reproducibility, and the limitations of predictive models.

I value the practical side of research as much as the questions that motivate it. I enjoy building analysis pipelines, writing tests, documenting methods, and making code easier for others to run and inspect. I see these practices as an essential part of sound computational research, rather than as tasks separate from the scientific process.

I would like to continue developing my research at the intersection of machine learning, neuroscience, and biomedical science. I am especially interested in reliable machine learning, computational approaches to biological data, and research software engineering. I am keen to work on problems where careful experimentation and sound software practices are as important as model performance.

My long-term goal is to contribute to research that is not only computationally effective but also transparent, reproducible, and useful to the wider scientific community.
